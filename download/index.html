v1.11.25

  • Treating pressure was being read against another channel’s scale — and most of it never showed up as bad data. Where the text reader loses the pressure scale, the app fell back to whichever scale it had found. On six charts that was the rate scale, so a pressure of 74 MPa exported as 18. On twenty-five more it was the concentration scale, which put the pressure about fifteen times too high — and the plausibility check then deleted it, so those pages quietly lost two channels each and looked like missing data rather than wrong data. Both are fixed at the source
  • The cause was a speck of ink. The pressure scale was being read — ten of its eleven numbers — but nine came back with the tick mark stuck to the end (“90-”, “80-”), and the reader only knew how to strip one off the front. That left two usable numbers, one short of what it takes to trust a scale. It now strips either end
  • Measured over 273 charts: 1,149 channels before, 1,201 after — 52 recovered, none lost, and the same ten pages fail either way. Every pressure channel in the corpus now reports a scale top of 90 or 100; before this there were scale tops reading 15 and 20
  • None of this was reported — it was found while checking the previous release. The pages affected are in filings that already looked like they had read correctly

Its elapsed-time labels print one decimal place and the reader dropped the point on five of the eight — “414.0” came back as “4140” — so five misreads outvoted three good ones, the stage measured 732 minutes against its neighbours' 64 to 84, and it was thrown out as impossible. 00016 goes from 28 stages to 29
  • Trican image charts: the rate trace was losing 41% of itself to a guard that mistook the curve for a gridline. On this layout the gridlines are drawn in the same colour as the curve they belong to, so the two can only be told apart by shape — a rule is hundreds of one-pixel dashes, a curve is a handful of long runs. It asks the row now
  • And both Trican proppant concentrations were missing from every page. The concentration axis prints only its zero, because it SHARES the rate axis at 100× — the same scale your other Trican layout has always used. Checked against the Conc Maximum each page prints for itself: 480.4 against a printed 483.1, 539.1 against 537.0, 553.8 against 552.2. Three channels became five
  • CalFrac charts headed “Zone #1” now read their zone. The hash sat where a space was expected, so 00525's 100 charts came through with no stage at all and merged into one. It now reads 50 zones, one sheet each, all four terms on every one
  • A stage whose clock runs backwards is now caught the moment you open the file. A banner over the graph and a mark against the stage in the list. Mislabelled times are hard to see one stage at a time and obvious across a well — 00495 has 66 stages and one of them is dated two days before the job starts. “Fix time” beside the flagged stage takes the corrected date and time and carries it into the graphs and the CSV; the sheet's original is kept, so you can undo it
  • FracView has a Sequence view — one line, the stage's position against the clock. Laid out correctly it is a single ascending staircase, so a stage whose clock is wrong leaves the line where nothing else would show it
  • FracView Real Time shows the waiting. The gap between one stage finishing and the next starting is drawn as its own shaded span with its length on it — “5h 30m · no readings” — instead of being coloured in as part of the stage before it
  • Coming back to FracView from another tab no longer resets the view to All and zooms out, and only Our Terms are drawn there now, as everywhere else
  • The chart type no longer rides along in the stage label: a stage called “1 Surface” exports as 1
  • Stacked charts are twice the height

v1.3.0

  • FracView would not open at all. It sat on “Waiting for the Lab…” on every well, on every report type, with the Lab right behind it holding the data. A button removed from the toolbar took the whole window down with it. Fixed, and Undo and Reset are back
  • Halliburton IFS charts now carry their date and time. Every stage on these was coming through with no date at all and a start time of 00:00:00 — on one 306-page report, all 41 of them. The date is printed under the chart's clock; we were only reading one of the two ways these reports write it. A stage that begins just before midnight is also no longer filed a day late
  • Schlumberger charts now carry their date. Where the report has no Zone Summary sheet, the date is taken from the Stimulation Service Report page for that interval, as you asked. One well went from every interval undated to all 52 dated
  • Schlumberger scales. The PRC charts were drawn against a rounded-off peak instead of the axis printed beside them, which is why the rate looked right and the pressure and concentration did not. They now read the printed axis — 0–90 where the sheet prints 0–90. Nothing you have already exported is wrong: the numbers were always read off the real axis. What changes is where the curve sits against the original sheet, and what the axis says
  • A flag report now quotes the axis the SHEET prints. It was quoting an internal measurement instead — that is why Liberty axes came back as −4.02…106.06 on a chart printing 0–100. Both numbers are shown when they disagree
  • A channel that goes missing now says why. On Trican charts two proppant concentration curves were absent from every stage with no explanation. They are traced fine; it is the concentration axis on that vintage we cannot read yet, so there is nothing to scale them against. The report says so now instead of leaving you to guess
  • Trican “Mainline Pressure” is now recognised as Treating Pressure, and BJ “SLR Rate” as Slurry Rate
  • FracView Real Time shows the waiting. The gap between one stage finishing and the next starting is drawn as its own shaded span with its length on it — “5h 30m · no readings” — instead of being coloured in as part of the stage before it
  • FracView Plot by row numbers the axis with the CSV's row number, so a feature on the chart can be found in the spreadsheet. The clock sits under it and the date below that
  • FracView lays the stages out in clock order even when the report prints them out of order
  • A strip between the two charts shows the stage number and the stage label, named the way the CSV names them
  • Tables: the eleven terms are always shown, and Map Term lets you point one at the right column when the heading is unfamiliar — remembered for every report from that provider

  • Please stop clicking Report on each empty file — a batch now reports ONCE. 141 reports arrived in three minutes from one run. When several files in a row come back with nothing, the app waits for the run to finish and sends a single report listing them all, so the hand-written ones stay findable. A file dropped on its own still reports on its own

Now it says which: that no page draws a plotted curve and there are no treatment charts to miss; or that the pages are pictures and need OCR; or that the charts are there but nothing readable names their axes. Several of the wells reported recently are the first kind — WellOps daily reports that hold tables and a wellbore schematic and never had a treatment chart

  • Liberty: filings from before the company renamed now read. Older reports print “Liberty Oilfield Services LLC” instead of “Liberty Energy” and nothing else on the page identifies the template, so whole wells came back empty. One goes from nothing to 84 charts across 41 stages
  • CalFrac/MView: a chart whose plot box is drawn in grey now reads. The reader only looked for a black one and reported “no plot frame found” on charts that are perfectly clear.   • Schlumberger: the ghost overlay works on vector charts, so an extracted curve can be checked against the printed one the same way it can for BJ. And the annulus pressure trace is no longer left out — it exports as its own channel rather than being folded into treating pressure
  • Halliburton IFS: one file per frac now gives its schedule. BJ Fracturing-Acidizing Treatment sheets are read: a 218-page well that reported no data gives a 124-row pumped schedule across 21 fracs, one row per pump step
  • A dropped file list no longer exports as it goes. It behaves like a dropped PDF — extract, show, and you decide when and where to export. A 67-file list used to write hundreds of CSVs into folders nobody had chosen, and on a read-only drive quietly put them in Downloads
  • Smaller things. The queue reads top to bottom, so the file being worked on is where you expect it. Reports carry each file’s full path, not just its name. The warning strip above a chart is back to carrying only the re-export notice instead of every diagnostic

v1.1.0

  • Halliburton IFS reports now give you their tables (report #330). These files showed their charts and no tables at all. The reader looked for Halliburton’s own IFS marker in the first 40 pages of a report and gave up; on 00056 that marker is on 129 of the 180 pages but the first one is page 52, so it was there in bulk and never seen. Because the charts are found a different way they kept working, which is why this looked like missing tables rather than a file that failed. 00056 now gives a 244-row Stage Summary and a 24-row Treatment Summary. The same 80-page limit in the Hal-1 reader is gone as well
  • Every table in a file now reaches your CSVs. Until now most of them did not. A Halliburton file that parses 14 tables was showing all 14 on screen, counting all 14 in “files ready”, and writing ONE — the rest were read correctly and thrown away on the way out, with nothing saying so. Please re-export any Halliburton or Schlumberger well you exported before this build; the tables you were missing were never in the files
  • Table CSVs are named after the table. They used to be numbered by position — -stages-table-13.csv — and the position moves between wells: on one Halliburton well number 13 is Cluster Data, and on the next well, which prints no Cluster Data, the same filename holds Stage Description. Anyone opening “table 13” from a folder of wells was combining different tables without being told. Now it is <well>-cluster-data-hal-1.csv, so a well that does not print a table simply has no file for it. If you have anything set up that reads the old numbered filenames, it will need pointing at the new names.
  • Canyon: the day-overview charts no longer merge into a stage. Canyon prints a plot per pumping day covering several stages, after the per-stage charts. Those were being read as extra copies of stages 1, 7, 15 and 22 — 7 and 8 hours long against a 68-minute stage — and the app drew them on top of the real stage, so stage 1 appeared on a 7-hour axis with the actual treatment squeezed into the left fifth. The page says which it is in its own Interval field: a real chart names a depth (“#1 - 3689.04m”), an overview names a range of stages (“#1 - 6”). Overviews are now left out and the reason is printed in the file’s notes. On 00011 that is 29 stages with four duplicates down to a clean 25
  • Where is the schedule. Tables are now grouped by what they ARE — Schedules first, then Summaries, then Logs, then everything else — so on a report that parses a dozen tables the pumped schedule is the first row instead of somewhere in the list. Schedules currently come from Halliburton IFS; Liberty’s Job Design and Halliburton Hal-1’s Actual Design are not read yet, so those wells will show an empty Schedules group for now
  • Extracted tables now sit ABOVE the scanned report pages in the Summary list, under a Source pages heading, instead of below them. On a report printing a dozen summary sheets the extracted data was off the bottom of the list
  • A Columns… button on the tables view. Choosing which columns your CSVs carry only opened from inside the Export menu; it is now beside the table you are actually looking at, and on the pop-out tables window
  • Liberty summary sheets show up on far more wells. They were only looked for when a Liberty chart had already been read, so a well whose plots did not come out showed no summary sheets either — even though those sheets are plain text sitting right there. The 2025-style Liberty reports were also unrecognised: they print Time Log, 24 Hour Summary and Completion Field Report where the older ones print Stimulation Summary. Across an 18-file check, summary sheets found went from 60 to 152
  • The app now tells you on the chart when an earlier export of that well is wrong. The v1.0.0 correction to Hydr Pressure and PFR-ZC FR CONC affects CSVs you already have. Opening an affected well now shows a notice naming the channel, both on the chart and on the file, so it cannot be missed by not reading these notes. It also says plainly that the error is not a constant factor, so an old export cannot be fixed by multiplying — re-export it

v1.0.0

  • Trican POST-FRAC SUMMARY reports now carry a date and a start time. They previously exported neither — 0 of 39 stages on 00005, 0 of 28 on 00317 — because the chart only prints elapsed minutes. Both are now read off the STAGE INFORMATION page that follows each chart. Across all 192 Trican files, 6,657 of 6,795 stages (98%) are dated, and the derived clock runs forwards on every one of them
  • Schlumberger fluid and proppant breakdowns are exported. Each Zone and Interval Summary sheet prints a per-stage fluid table (pad, slurry, spacer, flush, sweep — or the PUMPDOWN/FRAC steps) and a per-proppant-type table with designed vs placed tonnage. Neither was read before; 4,514 fluid rows and 1,805 proppant rows across 513 sheets in testing
  • Six Liberty 2025 files that produced nothing now extract in full. Their curves are drawn as filled ribbons rather than stroked lines, which the reader skipped entirely, and two of them title their charts "STG 1" instead of "Stage 1" so were never even detected. 01103, 01104, 01105, 01106, 01397 and 01398 now give 568 of 568 chart pages and 1.94 million samples, from zero
  • Correction to values in earlier builds — please re-export affected Liberty wells. A black curve with its own printed axis was being read off a coloured axis of the same unit. Where the two disagreed the numbers were wrong, not just imprecise. Across all 12 Liberty vector files, 403 of 901 pages carried a wrong value, and every one of them is a black series — no coloured channel moved anywhere. Hydr Pressure came out roughly 4.5x too high on 182 pages, pinned near the top of the chart. PFR-ZC FR CONC came out too LOW by a factor that depends on the range printed on that page: half on 147 pages, a third on 73, a quarter on 1. Do not try to correct an old export by multiplying — the factor is not the same on every page. Re-export the affected wells. Verified against the reports' own printed axes and the ink position on the page
  • Choose which columns your CSVs carry. A new Columns… button in the Export menu lists every column for the file on screen with a checkbox each. Save as default for this filetype remembers it per service provider, so unchecking a column once on a Liberty file applies to every Liberty export after it. UWI, STAGE and DATETIME always export, so a row can always be traced back
  • The export destination folder is now actually used. Setting a destination did nothing whenever the PDF's own folder was writable — the export reported success and the folder you chose stayed empty. The export panel now also names the folder the files really went to
  • A batch of files with no data files ONE report, not one each. "Report all empty" sends a single report listing every file and its reason
  • Flagging an error survives a GitHub outage. A 503 is retried, and if it still fails the message says the service is down and your report is still there to re-send — it is no longer discarded-looking
  • The app keeps a log. Errors used to go to a console window and nowhere else. There is now a log file (its location is printed when the app starts) with the full detail of any failure, which you can send with a report
  • What is still NOT fixed, so you know what to expect from the "no extractable data" batch. This release fixes 6 of those files, not the whole set — please do not re-run all of them expecting them to come out. Of the 42 reported files we can currently open, 6 are fixed above and the rest break down as: 19 files whose charts are drawn with every label converted to line art, so there is no text on the page for the reader to find at all — the curves are perfectly good and this is the next thing being built, and it is the biggest remaining group; 8 files (01151-01158) that contain no treatment charts in the first place — they are Ovintiv WellOps daily reports, all tables plus a single wellbore schematic, so "no data" is the correct answer for those and the job is to make the app say so instead of looking like it failed; and 9 more that are a mix. Separately, 152 of the reported files are not on either drive we have — they are all 2021 and 2022 filings and are presumably on the drive still to come

v0.9.10
