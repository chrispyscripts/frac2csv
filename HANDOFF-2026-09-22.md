## Two rules that earned their keep again

**Measure before and after on the real corpus, and count what you LOSE.**
Every reader fix this week was gated on a whole-file sweep reporting
channels gained, channels lost and values changed. Three separate fixes
looked right and lost channels — one lost 23 across three files — and the
sweep is the only thing that caught them.

**A test that asserts on a COPY of the logic is worse than no test.** Two
instances this week: `tests/test_lib1_refrac_letter.py` kept its own copy
of the stage regex (hoisted to `lib1._STAGE_TOKEN`), and a placeholder
`assertLessEqual(40, 40)` in `tests/test_hal_ruled_ladder.py` asserted
nothing. Replacing the latter with a real test broke CI, because the real
`_ocr_column` needs Pillow and the unit workflow installs only numpy and
pymupdf — it is now gated on PIL. **CI has no corpus drive and no PIL;
`python3 -m pytest tests -q` passing locally does not mean CI passes.**

## A defect shape that recurred three times this week

OCR fuses a mark onto a tick label, a strict numeric match drops it, the
ladder falls one short of a column, and a channel is silently mis-scaled
or lost.

- Liberty #713: the gridline welds to the label — `"80.00}"`, `"60.00}"`.
- IFS #715: gridlines run through the tick column so tesseract returns
  nothing at all from the strip.
- IFS v1.11.25: a tick speck welds to the END — `"90-"`, `"80-"` — and the
  existing rule stripped only a LEADING one.

If a channel is missing or mis-scaled, dump the tick labels before
anything else. `lib1._tick_num` / `_is_tick` and the symmetric strip in
`halliburton_ifs._axis_columns` are the two worked examples.

## Known and deliberately not fixed

- **8 phantom Slurry Prop Conc readings** on the 01367-family raster
  charts (of 21 originally). The axis title survives as ~20 lit pixels and
  the populations overlap at 0.59 ink-per-row with real pen ink, so
  clearing them would delete genuine readings. See the comment above
  `_furniture_cols`.
- **`meta.axes` under-reports the scale on ~15 channels** across four IFS
  files: the VALUES are right (read off the ruled frame) but the reported
  tick extent is short because OCR stopped reading the ladder. Distinct
  from mis-mapping, and much more common than it.
- **A "peak above its own reported axis" detector is NOT a safe guard as
  stated.** It was tried: 6 of 9 hits were short-axis reporting, not bad
  data, so as a refusal it deletes correct channels. It also cannot see
  the most common mis-mapping, because `lim` deletes those first. It might
  work stated against the RULED FRAME rather than the tick extent —
  unmeasured.
