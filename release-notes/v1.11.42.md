**STEP: no more spikes at the start of a chart (#799).**

On 00051's Chart 2 (page 150) the pressure enters the chart at its left edge from zero and climbs. The first few minutes read 18, 16, 1, 13, 13, 13, 21, 28, 16, 39, 39, 31 MPa. They now read 18, 19.6, 19.5, 19.6, 19.6, 19.5, 21, 28, 28, 31, 31, 31, 34.

Four different misreadings were behind those spikes, all where the trace opens partway through its rise:

- **The drop to 1.** One column read the bottom of the thick bar the line enters by, instead of where the line is.
- **The dip to 13.** The line's corner is drawn as a fat block, and the reader followed its bottom edge. The block's top edge stays at the entry level until the climb starts, so the line is read along it. Where the top edge itself drops first, the dip is on the page and stays (00052 p150, 00050 p145).
- **The drop back to 16.** One column read the foot of the climbing stroke instead of where the climb had got.
- **The jump to 39.** A speck where two gridlines cross was taken for the line. Such specks now go back onto the line, between the readings either side.

Each repair here reads no further on than the readings after it, so the top of a slanted stroke can't make a peak. A chart that opens on a pressure test held at the top of the frame, like 00051 p179, is left exactly as it was.
