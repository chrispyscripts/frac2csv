**Schlumberger: nine stages that had a time and no date now have both.** On 00021, 51 of 60 charts were dated and nine were not — the five re-treatments labelled "20 b", "23 B", "25 b", "34 B Hi Rate Flush" and "38 B", and both halves of intervals 50 and 51. Two different causes, and the same mistake behind each: a treatment was being looked up by its interval number alone.

**Five of them were never read.** The filing does print a service report for each — headed "// Interval 20b", "23B" and so on — but the pattern looking for them expected a space or a punctuation mark after the number, and "20b" has neither, so those five pages were passed over entirely. The chart could not have used them anyway, because "20 b" is not a number.

**The other four were read and then dropped on purpose.** Interval 50 prints two reports, the main treatment on 2019-02-28 and a High Rate Flush on 03-01, and interval 51 the same a day later. Read as one interval those two dates disagree, and the rule has always been to drop a date rather than guess which treatment it belongs to — a chart with no date shows a gap, a chart with the wrong date does not. That rule is unchanged. What changed is that the two are no longer read as one interval: the report says which treatment it is, the chart says which treatment it is, and they are matched on that.

**So the dates are now per treatment rather than per interval, which is the point.** "20" is 2019-02-08 and "20 b" is 2019-02-10 — two days apart. Had the old lookup managed to read them at all, it would have put both on the 8th.

**FracView: a stage that prints no clock no longer lands on top of the next one.** A stage with no time of its own is placed after the stage before it. Nothing checked whether it fitted: on 00100, stage 19 prints no clock and runs 45 minutes, stage 18 ends at 20:42 and stage 20 prints 21:19 — a 37-minute gap — so stage 19 was drawn eight minutes over stage 20's band. The same minutes under two stages, the same curve drawn twice, and nothing on screen to say which was which. A placed stage is now pulled back to end where the next printed stage begins. Where the gap is genuinely too short for the chart, as it is here, something has to overlap and it is now the stage you have already read rather than the one you are about to, and the error no longer carries forward into every stage after it. A stage that fits its gap is placed exactly as before.

**Which templates this release touches:** the **Schlumberger (SLB)** reader, and FracView. Every other template reads exactly as it did under v1.11.30.
