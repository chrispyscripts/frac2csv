**A STEP file that gave you nothing now gives you all four channels (#693).**

00344 exported chemical traces and none of the treatment data. Every one of its 32 surface charts — the plot that carries pressure, rate and both concentrations — was thrown away with "time axis unreadable".

The reader finds the plot frame by looking for the lowest strongly black line across the page. On that file the frame's bottom rule is about one and a half pixels tall, so it renders as two *pale* grey rows, while an interior gridline that lands squarely on one row renders darker than either. The reader took the gridline as the bottom of the plot, then looked for the clock labels in a strip that is inside the chart, and found none. The chemical chart on the same page draws its floor crisply and read fine, which is why the file looked half-alive rather than broken.

The frame's side rules do not have that problem — they are vertical, so half a pixel of width darkens one column instead of splitting across two. The bottom is now taken from where they stop.

**31 of 32 surface charts now read**, with Surface Pressure, Slurry Rate, Prop Conc and Btm Prop Conc.

**A Liberty channel that was being dropped is back (#692).** On 00914, Slurry Rate was missing from 35 of 45 treatment charts. Those pages have no usable text layer, so the legend and the axis numbers are read by OCR, and OCR recovered only two labels off the rate axis — 16 and 12. A tick has to look like one to be trusted, and the test for that is having two aligned neighbours of the same colour; with only two labels each has one, so both were discarded, the axis had no scale, and the channel went with it.

The page offers a second opinion. Every other axis on that sheet steps 65 points between labels, and those two are exactly that far apart. A pair sitting on the page's own gridlines, an even value step between them, is an axis. Slurry Rate now reads on 39 of 45; the remaining six lost their legend text to OCR entirely, so there is no name to attach.

Also: a rate unit read as "m?/min" now says m3/min, the same lost superscript already handled for kg/m3.
