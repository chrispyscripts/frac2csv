**Isolate Interpolation.** Some stretches of a curve were never drawn on the page. The reader recovers them from underneath whichever curve was painted over the top, and the chart has always shown them in pulsing black — but on a busy chart they are hard to pick out, and there was no way to do anything about one. There is now a button beside Flag Error that appears whenever a chart has any. It empties the graph of everything else and leaves the axes, the grid and those stretches alone on it. Click one to select it; it stops pulsing and turns red. Delete drops it, and every sample in it leaves the CSV and the Excel file with it. Undo puts it back. Clicking empty space still pans the chart, so nothing is in the way of reading it normally, and the selection is cleared when you open another stage.

**Those stretches are now drawn at all.** They were not. v1.10.0 shipped the pulsing black and it has never once appeared on a real file: the step that turns the reader's per-sample marks into spans for the chart failed on the very first channel it was given, every time, and took the whole file's read down with it. Any report with a curve hidden under another therefore failed to open in the Lab rather than showing the stretch. The line was written in an environment where it could not be run, so it was read and never executed. It is fixed, and there is now a test that fails against the old version, which is the part that was missing.

**A drive that is not plugged in says which drive.** Ten files in a row failed on the 16th with a page of technical text that came down to "no such file" — about files that were on a disk nobody had plugged in at the time. Every one of them was present the next day. The Lab now checks whether the volume itself is there and says "For-Chris-CnC-1TB is not mounted. Plug the drive back in and try again — the file is on it, it is not missing." Windows drive letters read the same way.

**Closing a tab in the middle of a read is no longer an error.** It used to be recorded as a crash, with two pages of stack trace, because the Lab tried to reply to a window that had already gone. It is one line now.

**Two windows no longer write into one log file.** Opening two Labs in the same second gave them the same log, and their lines arrived spliced into each other mid-sentence — in exactly the file meant to be readable when something goes wrong. Each window keeps its own now.
