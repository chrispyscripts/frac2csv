**STEP: the false spike before a pressure rise is gone (#799).**

You were right: the spikes just before the pressure comes up are the app's, not the chart's. At pump start the pressure goes almost straight up, and the thick line covers several columns of pixels, each inked from the bottom part way up. The app takes one reading per column. It was taking the top of one column and the bottom of the next, so the export showed the pressure jumping, dropping back to zero, and rising again.

A column that reads back where the move began, while its own ink reaches as far as the move has already got, is now read from the leading end. 00051's 14:31 stage goes 18 → 20.7 → 27.4 → 29.7 instead of 17.1, 7, 0.7, 0.7, 13.4. The same applies to sharp falls.

Real spikes and dips stay, including:

- a drawn dip after the top (00051 p156's 30 → 23);
- an overshoot at the top of a fall (00200 p189's pressure tops out at 80 MPa on the stroke it falls by);
- a needle during a move (00006 p154's rate to 13.4 on its way down);
- a peak hidden under another curve (00163 p207's pressure at the top of the frame, under the rate).

Checked on 32 STEP filings (the 12 BCER 2026 files and 20 older AER ones), 9,538 channels:

| | v1.11.38 | v1.11.39 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| channels read identically | | 5,528 |
| samples off their curve's trend | 26,593 | 20,656 (−22%) |
| V-shaped dips | 17,675 | 16,332 (−8%) |

No pressure or rate maximum dropped. The only maxima that moved by more than 2% are chemical concentrations: two whose curves run to the top of the frame now read the top (00187 p190 9.6 → 10.0, 00540 p152 4.7 → 5.0), and a few on very small scales by 0.1.

Still to do: on a few rises the fix repairs one column, and the next still reads the bottom of the stroke, so a smaller dip is left (00048 p130's first rise reads 20, 21.5, then 7). Telling that apart from a real notch drawn just after a rise needs more work.
