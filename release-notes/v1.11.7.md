**Trican stage 1 was missing up to three hours of itself, and now it isn't.**

On the file we have been comparing, stage 1's own STAGE INFORMATION sheet says it ran 296.8 minutes, from 8:31 PM to 1:28 AM. Its chart page only plots the last 107 of those minutes — the page's Elapsed Time axis starts at 195, not at 0. The first 195 minutes of that stage are printed on one page in the whole report: the CONTINUOUS overview at the back.

We were reading the stage chart and nothing else, so stage 1 came out 96 minutes long and about 200 minutes of a real stage were simply not there. That is the truncation you have been reporting on Trican, and it was right.

Stage 1 now reads 290 minutes and starts at 8:32 PM. The minutes that come off the overview page are drawn there at roughly a minute per pixel instead of a second, so they are coarser than the rest of the stage — the stage says so in its warnings rather than pretending the join is invisible. Nothing is spliced unless the stage's own sheet says the stage ran longer than its chart, and never further back than that sheet says.

**The nameless last "stage" is gone.** That was the CONTINUOUS page, the whole job re-plotted end to end with no stage number. It was being kept precisely because it held stage 1's missing minutes; now that those minutes are on stage 1, it is dropped. On that file the export goes from 1,179 minutes of stages plus a 1,180-minute duplicate of all of them, to 1,179 minutes once.

**You can now delete a stage.** Edit stage is a proper dialog instead of a bare text box, and it carries "Delete this stage" with the stage's own size beside it, so you can see whether you are removing a page that is not a stage or thousands of rows of real data. It takes the stage out of the list and out of the CSV. Nothing is removed from the extraction and Restore puts it back; the list keeps a standing line saying how many are deleted, so it cannot be forgotten about.

**Type a Y-axis value instead of hunting for it with the slider.** Each channel in FracView now has a box under its slider holding the top of that channel's axis in its own units. Type 80 and the axis is 80. The slider is still there and the two stay in step. Empty the box to go back to the channel's own maximum.

**The readout above the FracView chart has its own space now.** It was drawn on top of the plot, so reading the row number cost you the first thirty pixels of every curve — usually the peaks.

**Releasing the trim tool no longer jumps you to stage 1.** The cut was being made correctly and then the stage list rebuilt, which threw the selection back to the first stage and took FracView with it. The span you had just cut ended up hours off screen, so the trim looked like it had done nothing. It had.
