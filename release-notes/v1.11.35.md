**Canyon: no more false spike at the start of the Slurry Rate (#780, #781).** The Slurry Rate on Canyon charts is the black Combined WH curve, and black is also the ink of the axes. On these sheets the tick marks on the value axis run right up to the edge of the plot, and the app read them as points on the black curve. So it started at the top of its axis and fell to zero over the first 45 seconds: 8 m³/min on 00009, 12 on 00371, which the PDF does not draw. Those tick marks also set each chart's start, 10 to 50 seconds before the plot begins.

**What changes on Canyon charts, said plainly.** Across the 97 Canyon files, the spike is gone from the black curves, and every chart now starts where its plot begins, a median of 25 seconds later than before. 00009 stage 1 starts 11:45:51 under a first label of 11:46, where it used to say 11:45:11. Apart from that, every curve is what it was, within 1% of its range. The same charts and stages come out, and where a chart runs on into the next stage, the cut moves by those same few seconds.

**BJ: a chart that starts just before midnight is dated the right day.** A BJ chart took its date from its first clock label and its time from where the curves start. A stage whose pumps came on at 23:58, under a first label of "00:00", came out dated the next day at 23:58, a full day late. On the files checked, 13 charts move to the right day, all of them starting in the last minutes before a midnight label.

**BJ: a chart over the end of a short month reads.** The app counted every month as 31 days, so "Nov-30 23:45" to "Dec-01 00:00" looked like two days. 01793 stage 11 read as starting 19:34 and lasting 837 minutes. It runs 22:43 to just after midnight, 79 minutes, and now reads that way. A chart over the end of April, or of February, failed outright, and now reads too, in leap years and common years. Across 6,367 BJ chart pages, only the 01793 pages and one 01116 stage change.
