**A stage is no longer cut before it stops pumping. This is the truncation.**

You were right, and here is what was happening.

Every Trican chart overlaps the next one, and the app cut each stage where the next chart's window opens. That assumed the chart runs on *past* the next stage's start. It is the other way round: **the next chart opens about eleven minutes early and re-plots the end of the stage before it as its own lead-in.** Both arrangements look identical to a correlation — they are literally the same samples — so the overlap could not tell them apart.

The data can. On the file we have been comparing, all 27 trimmed stages were being cut in the middle of proppant-laden slurry, and the eleven minutes handed to the next stage began at exactly the concentration the stage was cut at and fell to zero, with pressure going from about 60 MPa to 27 and rate to 2. That is a tail-in, a flush and a shutdown. A stage never *starts* at 344 kg/m³ and ramps down to zero.

Stage 1 was the clearest case. Cut at 96.0 minutes while pumping 142 kg/m³; it flushed at 100, pumped to 103, and shut down at 104.1. All of that — the tail-in, the flush, and the ISIP — was being filed under stage 2.

Now the cut is carried forward to where the stage actually stops, keeping the shutdown with the stage it belongs to. On that file: **27 of 27 stages now cut after their own shutdown, none of them mid-proppant.** Stage 1 keeps 105.5 of its 107 printed minutes instead of 96, and the chart stacks against the printed page with only the genuine hand-over greyed at the very end.

Nothing is claimed back without evidence. The stage must still be carrying proppant where the cut falls — if it has already flushed, or is on pad and ramping up, the next stage really is starting there and the cut stays where it was. A chart with no proppant channel is left alone entirely.

Checked against the previous build on STEP and Liberty files: output identical. This reaches Trican charts and nothing else.
