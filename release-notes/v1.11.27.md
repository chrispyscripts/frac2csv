**Smoothing you control, on the chart.** A traced curve carries two kinds of noise that want opposite treatment, and until now the app picked for you. There is now a filter box above the chart: choose one, and the slider beside it sets how hard it works — live, on every curve at once, so you can see exactly what it costs before you keep it.

**Spikes and staircases are not the same problem.** A spike is the tracer following the wrong ink for a moment — a gridline, or the fringe of the curve next to it. A staircase is the pixel grid quantising a smooth ramp into steps. Smoothing a spike does not remove it, it spreads it into a broad hump, so the recommended setting strips the spikes first (Hampel) and only then smooths what is left (Savitzky-Golay). Both are offered on their own as well, along with a blunt median and a robust LOESS for trend.

**A step stays a step and a peak keeps its height.** That is why Savitzky-Golay rather than a rolling average: it fits a small polynomial rather than averaging, so a pump shutdown stays vertical, a breakdown pressure keeps its number, and nothing shifts in time. The slider reports the window it is using in samples, so the setting can be written down and repeated.

**Save it once for a service provider.** The Save button offers three scopes: this stage, every stage in this file — including the ones you have not opened — or the default for this provider's future files. A digitiser's noise belongs to the plotter, not to the well, so a setting dialled in once on a STEP report is what every later STEP report should open with. A stage you have set yourself always keeps its own setting.

**Nothing is filtered unless you ask.** The filter starts off, and off means the curve is exported exactly as it was read. What the chart shows is what the CSV carries, so there is never a question of which one is right.

**Fix a stretch now takes two clicks instead of a dragged box.** Click the start of the bad stretch, click the end, then Apply — so you can see and re-pick the two points before anything is written, and a third click starts over. Undo is its own button. The repair itself is unchanged: the curve between the points is read again off the page underneath.
