**STEP charts: no more broken lines, and no more lost chart ends.** You said STEP 1 loses a lot of data at the ends of the charts. It did, and the curves also broke up partway through. Both are fixed, and STEP charts now come through as unbroken lines from the first ink on the chart to the last.

**The ends of the chart are kept.** At shutdown the pressure falls almost straight down, and that stroke is a one-pixel line the reader mostly misses. What is left of it (the lower part of the fall, the hold, the drop to zero) comes through as small, dense pieces with nothing joining them to the curve. The reader was treating those pieces like the FracPro logo printed inside the chart and deleting them. On 00100 page 197 the pressure ink runs 641 seconds past where the export stopped, which is the whole shutdown and where ISIP is read. The reader now keeps a tall piece that starts just past the end of the curve and below where the curve stopped, because a shutdown only goes down.

Checked on 20 AER STEP filings, 7,164 channels, against 1.11.36:

| | channels |
| --- | --- |
| identical, sample for sample | 6,859 |
| longer at the end of the chart, 30,271 s (8.4 h) in all | 304 |
| shorter anywhere | 0 |
| changed where 1.11.36 had no reading of its own | 8 (60 samples) |

Those last 8 are short stretches at a shutdown where 1.11.36 had no ink reading and drew a straight line or a flat value across. The reader now reads the ink there. Every sample 1.11.36 actually read is unchanged.

**Broken stretches are bridged.** A STEP chart is a picture, read one column of pixels at a time, and a curve goes blank wherever its ink can't be seen. That happens under another curve painted over it (Btm Prop Conc under Prop Conc), along the frame's bottom line where a stopped rate or a bled-off pressure rests, and through a near-vertical shutdown. The chart draws an unbroken line through all of these, and now the export does too:

- **A gap with a reading on both sides** is filled with a straight line between the two readings.
- **Before the first reading and after the last,** the curve is carried as far as the chart has ink at all, and no further. The empty margin after the data stays empty.
  - A curve resting on the floor stays there.
  - A curve that stops in mid-air follows the page. If the curve had settled at that height and the page keeps that height inked, the curve is still there under another pen and holds its reading: on 00108 page 282 the orange Chem Conc runs at 0.1 under the yellow Bio Conc for 1.8 hours. A single short piece isn't held, because it may be another pen misread. Where the page shows nothing at that height, the curve has gone down to the floor.
  - A pressure goes to its shut-in level instead: its last reading where it arrived there level, or the low it reached at the stroke.
- **Off the top of the scale stays blank,** as you asked in #97.

On 25 STEP filings (2,465 charts, image and vector), the broken stretches came to about 780 hours of curve. None are left, apart from 12 stretches (14 minutes) where a curve runs off the top of its scale.

**Everything filled in is marked as deduced, not read.** In the Lab it pulses. "Isolate Interpolation" shows those stretches on their own, and any one you don't trust can be selected and deleted, which takes it out of the export too. Each file's notes say how much was filled.

**The stacked window now follows the interpolation.** It used to keep showing what it had when you turned "Isolate Interpolation" on or off, or deleted or undid a stretch, and it drew deduced stretches like measured ones. Now it refreshes on all three. It draws deduced stretches dashed and says how much of each channel is deduced, and with Isolate on it shows only those stretches, as the Lab does.

**Which templates this release touches:** STEP (image and vector charts), and the Lab's stacked window. Every other template reads exactly as it did under v1.11.36.
