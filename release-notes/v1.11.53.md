**00935 (#810), the three charts v1.11.52 left open:**
- **Stage 3 was clocked six hours late.** Its axis prints 03:04 to 03:32, and every label read right, yet the chart was clocked at 09:02. The clock reader STEP shares with Trican breaks a tie by assuming a 9 can read as a 3, which happens in Trican's font but not STEP's. STEP now keeps the labels as read: 03:02.
- **Stage 6 lacked its concentrations and clock.** Both right-hand axis titles read as "rate", so the 0-1200 ladder was refused as a rate axis, and both concentrations went with it. A rate-titled ladder that reaches the hundreds now fills the concentration slot when nothing else has. The clock labels had split at the colon; they are now also read as one strip when the split pieces fit nothing. Stage 6 reads Surface Pressure, both concentrations and Slurry Rate, from 05:00.
- **Stage 14.1 did not read.** A note box in the plot's corner hid the top of its right-hand frame, so the plot was never found. Then its clock labels lost their leading 1 ("5:08" beside "15:12") and fitted a 37-hour chart. Both are fixed: 15:07, 15½ minutes, all four channels.

00935 now reads 18 charts, up from 16; stage 16 (p69) is new too. Its clocks run in stage order: 02:21, 03:02, 03:47, 04:25, 05:00, 05:34 and on.

**Time labels placed by their own position.** A STEP chart's clock is fitted to its time labels. Each label is now placed where the OCR found that word, not at the centre of the piece of the strip it was cut from. That piece can take in a neighbouring word: 00935 p69 ran 73 minutes for the 69 its labels span, and now reads 69.8. Across the test set the clock's duration matches the chart's printed span as closely as before, within a second or two. Lengths move by a few samples on most STEP charts, and 00005 p146's chemical chart now clocks at 116 minutes instead of a garbled 4,573.

**00185: chemical charts under the "Offset Analysis" bar.** p33 and p45 print the stage's chemical chart in the treatment chart's place, under the "Offset Analysis" bar. Its additives were named Prop Conc and the like; they now get their own names. The book's "DGB Analysis" bar heads fleet charts and reprinted charts alike, so each chart's legend, not its title, decides how it is read.
