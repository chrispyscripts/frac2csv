**The CSV was blank where the chart drew a line. A pen resting at zero is a reading of zero.**

When the pumps are off, the rate trace lies along the bottom rule of the plot frame. The tracer finds no ink there, so the channel came out empty — and empty is what reached the CSV, while the chart drew a line along the floor. Opened in another program, those blanks look exactly like the data loss we have spent this week chasing.

On the file we have been comparing, stage 1's Slurry Rate was **46% empty**, the longest run 33.7 minutes. It decays to 0.089 m³/min going into that run and resumes at 0.107 coming out, on a 0 to 12.5 axis. It did not go anywhere — it was shut in.

Those stretches are now filled with the floor they rested on. Stage 1's Slurry Rate goes from 53% present to **99%**, and the 33.7 minute hole reads 0.098 m³/min instead of nothing.

**Only a resting pen, never a lost trace.** A gap is filled only when the readings on *both* sides sit within 2% of the axis floor, and only when the chart printed an axis to measure that floor against. A gap that starts and ends at pumping rate is a genuine loss and is left alone — filling that would be inventing a treatment. Nothing before the pen starts or after the flush is touched.

**And every filled sample is marked.** They draw as the pulsing black stretches, Isolate Interpolation finds them, and you can delete them. The flag report still says the gap was there and what kind it was.
